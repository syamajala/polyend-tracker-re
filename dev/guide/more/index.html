    More features¶

    Note names¶

    Config > General > Note names: Sharps / Flats / Auto. Auto spells notes the way the project's scale does, modes included: C Mixolydian shows B♭, not A♯. Display only.

    Piano pads¶

    Config > General > Pad layout: Stock / Piano. Piano turns the pads into two keyboards that light up as ones, laid out as on grid controllers: each pair of rows is a keyboard, with the white keys along the lower row and the black keys in the row above, each black key over the white key to its right.

    ·  C# D# ·  F# G# A# ·  C# D# ·  F#     upper keyboard
C  D  E  F  G  A  B  C  D  E  F  G
·  C# D# ·  F# G# A# ·  C# D# ·  F#     lower keyboard
C  D  E  F  G  A  B  C  D  E  F  G
    • Each keyboard spans an octave and a fifth; the upper one is the same keyboard an octave higher.
    • The pads marked · (over C and F, where a piano has no black key) are dark and play nothing.
    • The bottom-left C is the C of the project's root note octave. To play higher or lower, change the root note.
    • The lights show the keyboard. With a scale picked, the scale's keys light at one level, black or white, the root brighter; keys outside the scale stay playable but are barely lit, so the scale shows on the keyboard. In Chromatic the lights draw a piano: white keys lit, black keys a little dimmer, the root brightest.
    • It works wherever the pads play notes: the pattern editor's Note column, Sample Playback, the instrument pages, the synth browser, the Sample Editor and the recorder. In the pattern editor the step's note (and, with Pads preview, the notes on the current line) lights at full brightness.
    • Pads that do something else keep their own lights: the Instr and FX columns, Performance, the synth edit page, and Beat Slice instruments (one pad per slice).
    • Stock is the default and keeps the original layout. The setting is global, not saved with the project.

    Chord hints¶

    With a scale picked (Config > Project Settings), hold a key on the piano pads and the notes of its chord light up. In A minor, hold E: on the upper keyboard E, G and B light (Em, the chord on E in the key), on the lower keyboard E, G# and B (E major, its alternate). Press the lit keys to play the chord. The screen shows Em v: E G B / E V: E G# B while you hold it.

    • The upper keyboard shows the chord built from the scale's own notes (every other note of the scale: root, 3rd, 5th), with its number in the key (v).
    • The lower keyboard shows the alternate major / minor chord on the same root: the 3rd moved a semitone, so a minor chord becomes major and the other way round. Some of these are common outside notes (E major in A minor is the V chord of harmonic minor), others just a colour to try. Diminished and augmented chords have none.
    • With Note names on Auto the notes are spelled from the key, one letter per note of the scale: in F# major the chord on E# is E#dim: E# G# B, not F. With Sharps or Flats they follow that setting.
    • In a mode the hints show the mode's own chords (in D Dorian, G is major: G IV). Song mode progressions play modes as their parallel major or minor key, so a chord from a progression can differ from the hint (i VI in D Dorian is Dm Bb).
    • No hints in Chromatic or in scales of fewer than 5 notes, or where the pads do something else (Performance).

    Chord entry¶

    In the pattern editor's edit mode (stopped), pads pressed while another note pad is still held make a chord: the first note goes on the cursor's step and the cursor moves on as usual; each further note goes on the same step, one track to the right. Hold the chord's pads together, release them, and play the next chord.

    • With a selection over several tracks, the chord fills the selected tracks on the selection's first step instead, and the cursor stays.
    • One Undo removes the whole chord.
    • A track that can't play the current instrument (a MIDI track for a sample instrument) ends the chord there.
    • Live recording (Rec while playing) already spreads a chord over the next free tracks.

    Slices to pattern¶

    Chop a break, then press one key to get a pattern that plays it. Without this you would enter every slice by hand on the right step before the break even plays straight; with it, flipping the break is plain pattern editing.

    For example, with a 2-bar drum break:

    1. Load it into an instrument. On Sample Playback, pick Slice mode and press F1 Auto: it cuts at each hit, say into 12 slices.
    2. Open the More page (F6). F4 Steps shows 32 (2 bars at the song tempo); turn the encoder if it guessed wrong.
    3. With the pattern cursor on an empty track, press F5 To Pattern. The track gets one note per slice, on the step where its hit is: slice 1 as C2, slice 2 as C#2, and so on.
    step  1    2    3    4    5    6    7  …
note  C2   ---  C#2  ---  D2   ---  D#2
      kick      hat       snare     hat
    1. Press Play: the break plays as recorded, in time. Now edit the track as any other: change a note to swap a hit (slice 3 → slice 1 puts a kick where the snare was), delete notes for space, or repeat the snare slice for a roll.

    On the Sample Playback screen (Slice or Beat Slice mode), the More page (F6) has:

    Key Does
    Steps (F4) How many steps the sample lasts. The guess is its length at the song tempo, rounded to 1, 2, 4 or 8 bars; turn the encoder if it guessed wrong (a 2-bar break at 92 BPM in a 140 BPM song reads as 4 bars).
    To Pattern (F5) Writes the slices on the pattern's current track (the one the cursor was on).
    • Slice i goes on the step nearest where it starts, as note 24 + i, with the steps in between empty, so each slice plays on to the next one. The trigs repeat every Steps steps to the end of the track.
    • The track is cleared first; it asks when the track had steps. Undo in the pattern editor brings them back.
    • A Slice instrument turns Beat Slice, so each note plays its own slice.
    • At another song tempo the slices play at their own speed and pitch (as REX loops do): a faster song cuts each slice short at the next trig, a slower one leaves a short gap after it. The pattern keeps its length; a sample longer than the track is placed once, cut at the track's end.
    • Slice first: Auto (F1) for drum breaks, Evenly (F2) for loops in time.

    Instrument library¶

    Keep a sample instrument you've set up (its sample, play mode, slices, envelopes, filter, effects…) and use it again in any project. On the Instrument Effects page (Instrument Params, then More):

    • Save (F7) opens the keyboard with the instrument's name; Save writes it to Instruments/<name>.pti on the card (into the folder you last opened in Load). An existing name asks before replacing it.
    • Load (F6) lists Instruments/: folders first, then instruments. F8 opens a folder or loads the instrument into the current slot (replacing it); Left goes up a folder (at Instruments/ it closes the list); F7 cancels. Folders can be nested as deep as you like.

    • The files are stock .pti instruments: the Sample Loader lists them too, and stock firmware loads them.
    • A long instrument streamed from the card is copied in full first, so Save can take a moment.
    • An instrument that doesn't fit in the free sample memory isn't loaded ("Not enough sample memory"); the slot keeps what it had.
    • A multisample instrument saves as a Slice instrument (one zone per slice, as stock firmware sees it): the zones' root notes and the Multi play mode are kept with the project, not in the .pti.

    Memory¶

    Config > Memory shows what fills the Tracker's memory, for when something won't load. For example, with DX7 on a synth slot, turning on the last card effects can fail with "Card memory full". The page shows DX7 taking 18 KB of the card memory, so you know to turn off an effect or switch the slot to a built-in synth.

    • Card modules: the card synths and effects that are loaded (DX7, Echo …), what uses them (synth, slot 2, synth, P3 for a synth preset, effect) and their size. Ones that are wanted but not in are listed too, with why: loads at Stop, no room (red), didn't load.
    • Sample memory: the samples, plus what is borrowed from it: card effects' buffers (Echo's 48 KB line) and the space held back while resampling or during a tape stop.
    • The bar turns red at 90 %. The page is read-only.

    When a card module doesn't fit, the notice names the biggest one loaded, e.g. "Echo: Card memory full (DX7 18.3 KB)", and the FX bank shows how much card memory is used. In the simulator the card modules are built in, so the page lists only the sample memory.

    Colour themes¶

    Make a theme in Tracker CE's theme editor, press Export Theme, and copy the .json file to the Themes folder on the card. Pick it under Config > General > Display theme, after the three built-in themes. Themes made for Tracker CE work on OpenTracker, and the other way round.

    • Export theme (Config > General) saves the theme you're using, built-in or not, as Themes/<name>.json. Import that into the editor to start from it. An existing file is never overwritten.
    • A theme draws in CE's style: flat panels without fades, and the band across the pattern only on the playing row while the song plays. The built-in themes keep the stock look.
    • Colours a file leaves out come from its base (Original, Monochrome or Anniversary). Keys that have no counterpart on OpenTracker (CE's mini menu and on-screen keyboard) are ignored, and they aren't written on export.
    • Text set to its own background colour (unreadable) falls back to the base's colours.
    • Twelve example themes are in themes/ in the repository, among them Halloween (contributed by AnthonyBerg1983).
    • The old key = value text themes (.txt) are no longer read.

    The 2.x features (from upstream)¶

    The 2.x line brings the newer Tracker features to the original hardware. In short:

    Tracker Mini project compatibility¶

    • Projects are saved in the Tracker 2.2.1 format: 16-track patterns, the three synth slots, track names and mutes for tracks 13–16.
    • Synth patches use the Mini's .patch files, stored with the project and in a library at Patches/<ENGINE>/. All 299 Mini factory patches load and save back unchanged.

    16 tracks¶

    • Tracks 13–16 work like MIDI tracks 9–12: notes, MIDI channels, CC A–F, pitch bend, velocity, mute and solo. Performance Play mode, Pattern length and Step repeater act on them too.
    • Pattern pages through all 16 tracks; Song shows a scrolling 12-track window. Fill, copy and paste work across all 16.

    Synth engines (instruments S1–S3)¶

    • Three synth slots, played from any track as instruments S1–S3.
    • Engines: ACD (acid bass and lead, 24 dB and ladder-style filters, glide), VAP (virtual-analog poly, morphing oscillators, hard sync, 15 filter types, a 6-slot modulation matrix), PERC (a drum kit), FAT (unison supersaw), WTFM (two-operator wavetable FM), and in this fork DX7 (from the card).
    • Macros: FX columns A–F drive macros 1–6, and Volume drives macro 7.
    • Voices: a shared pool of 4 by default, adjustable in Config > General > Synth voices (1–8). If the audio gets close to its limit, only synth voices are cut; sample tracks never are.
    • Screens after the Mini's layout: Synth Parameters, Synth Macros, Edit Patch (sections on the pads; Save, Save As, Init), the Load Patch popup with Preview, and the synth level columns on Global Mixer page 3.
    • Exports include separate stems synth_1..3.wav.

    Export sample rate¶

    Config > General > Export sample rate: 44.1 kHz (default) or 44117 Hz. The Tracker's audio runs at 44,117 Hz. At 44.1 kHz, exported songs, patterns, stems and renders are converted to a standard 44,100 Hz WAV, so they play in time in any DAW. 44117 Hz writes the Tracker's own rate, as the original firmware does.

    Radio stereo¶

    Config > General > Radio stereo: Off (default) or On. On records the built-in radio in stereo, and the Sample Recorder's source list shows Radio ST. Off keeps it mono, as the original firmware does: mono is cleaner on weak stations and doubles the recording time.

    Stereo samples¶

    Stereo WAVs keep both channels and play in stereo in one-shot, loop, slice and beat-slice modes; pan works as balance.

    CPU meter¶

    Config > General > CPU meter: Auto, Always or Off shows the audio load in the title bar.

    USB Storage mode¶

    Config > USB > USB Storage: On shows the SD card on a connected computer as a USB drive, as on the Tracker Mini, so projects, samples and patches can be copied without taking the card out.

    • Playback stops and the project is saved first. Keep the power and the cable connected while files are copied.
    • To finish, eject the drive on the computer, then press Exit (or set the row back to Off). The project reloads from the card.
    • It's always Off after power-on. About 1 MB/s (full-speed USB). USB MIDI and the serial log keep working.
    • On Windows the Tracker may appear once as a new device (new COM/MIDI port).
